# 3-Question ROI Math — Sales Call Framework

Saved 2026-11-23, pasted by Demetrius.
Walk through out loud in under 2 minutes. Owner supplies their own numbers so the math feels self-discovered, not pitched. Recovering even **2–3 missed leads/month** covers a **$7,500** Tier 2 build.

## The 3 questions

### Q1: What is one average customer or job worth to you?

- **Ask:** "Roughly speaking, when a new customer books or buys from `[Company Name]` — whether it's a job, a contract, a treatment plan, or a project — what's a typical ticket or first-year gross value?"
- **Why:** Establishes unit value ($V$) in their own vocabulary without guessing.

### Q2: What is your rough close rate on qualified inquiries?

- **Ask:** "Out of every 10 people who actually reach out and get a real quote or consultation, how many turn into paying customers — say 2, 3, or 5?"
- **Why:** Converts raw inquiries into realistic closed deals ($C$).

### Q3: How many warm leads slip through or cool off each month?

- **Ask:** "Between slow mobile forms, missed calls when you're tied up, and follow-ups that get pushed to tomorrow, how many decent inquiries a month do you figure quietly slip away or go with someone faster?"
- **Why:** Anchors on a conservative recovery target ($R$ = just **2 to 3 recovered leads/month**) rather than promising to fix every leak.

## Live call math

```
Recovered Revenue / Month = Recovered Leads × Close Rate × Avg. Deal Value
Payback Period (Months) = $7,500 / Monthly Recovered Gross Revenue (or Gross Profit)
```

## Example walkthroughs

| Scenario | Avg. Deal / Job Value | Close Rate | Leads Recovered / Mo | Closed Deals / Mo | Monthly Revenue Added | Time to Cover $7,500 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Conservative Service / Trade | $1,500 | 50% (1 in 2) | 3 leads | 1.5 deals | $2,250 / mo | ~3.3 months |
| Mid-Ticket B2B / Healthcare | $3,000 | 40% (2 in 5) | 2.5 leads | 1.0 deal | $3,000 / mo | 2.5 months |
| Higher-Ticket Project / Retainer | $5,000 | 30% (3 in 10) | 3 leads | 0.9 deals | $4,500 / mo | ~1.7 months |

## Word-for-word call script

> "Let's test the napkin math on that for a second — tell me if my numbers are off:
> 1. You mentioned a typical customer is worth around **`$[V]`**.
> 2. If you close roughly **`[C, e.g., 40%]`** of real inquiries, every **2.5** solid leads you convert equals about **1** new customer — around **`$[V]`** in top-line revenue.
> 3. Right now, with `[mention their exact bottleneck, e.g., slow mobile intake and manual follow-up]`, you estimate **`[L, e.g., 8–10]`** inquiries a month get delayed or leak.
>
> You don't need to recover all ten for this to pay off. Suppose automating speed-to-lead, booking reminders, and cleaner web intake simply saves **2 to 3 of those leads a month** — that's roughly **`$[Monthly Added]`** in additional monthly revenue. Against a **$7,500** Tier 2 build, that pays back the setup in about **`[X]` months** — before even counting the hours of manual follow-up your team gets back every week.
> Does that math feel conservative to you, or are you actually losing more than that?"

## Tips

- **Use gross margin with financially sharp SBA owners:** SBA borrowers often track net/gross margin closely. If gross margin is 60% on a $2,500 job ($1,500 gross profit per job), show the math on **gross profit** so payback stays honest and credible.
- **Let them push back lower:** If an owner says "Honestly, maybe only 2 leads a month," smile and say, "Even at just 2 leads a month, that's still `[X]` extra deals — let's see what that looks like." Lowering your own claim builds huge trust.
- **Tie time saved to real labor cost:** If they spend 6 hours/week on manual follow-up/re-entry and value their time at $75/hr, that's another **~$1,950/month** in reclaimed capacity alongside the recovered leads.

## Use in the call flow

Use in Phase 3 (Quantify Cost, 6:00–10:00) of the 15-min discovery call, or in the Scope & Workflow Map session before presenting tiers. Their numbers, their math — then Tier 2 payback in ~1.7–3.3 months on the examples above.
