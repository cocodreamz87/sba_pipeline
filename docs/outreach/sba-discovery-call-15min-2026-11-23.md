# 15-Minute Discovery Call — SBA Owner-Operators

Saved 2026-11-23, pasted by Demetrius.
Applies to all SBA tracks (A/B/C), after Email 1 + Loom / call follow-up.

Principle: succeeds or fails on **respect for their clock, diagnostic precision, and plain-English clarity**. No 40-slide agency deck, no jargon about LLMs or headless architectures. They want to know if you understand where leads and hours are leaking today and how you fix it cleanly.

## Time-boxed agenda

| Time | Stage | Core Objective |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–2:00 | Frame & Permission | Set a hard 15-minute ceiling, confirm what they saw (or didn't see), earn permission to ask direct questions. |
| 2:00–6:00 | Current-State & Bottleneck Diagnosis | Map how an inquiry/customer arrives, where manual work happens, what slips through when they are busy. |
| 6:00–10:00 | Quantify Cost & Ideal State | Put a real number on response lag, manual admin hours, or missed bookings — define what "clean" looks like. |
| 10:00–13:00 | Tailored Web + Automation Blueprint | Connect frontend conversion + backend workflow automation directly to their exact bottlenecks. |
| 13:00–15:00 | Next Step / Scope Decision | Either book a focused scope/audit walkthrough or cleanly disqualify if there is no fit. |

## Word-for-word script

### Phase 1: Frame & Permission (0:00–2:00)

> "Hey `[First Name]`, thanks for making time — I know your day is packed, so I have a hard stop at 15 minutes so we don't eat into your afternoon.
> Quick picture of how I'd like to use the time: spend about six or seven minutes looking at how leads and day-to-day requests come in right now and where manual work slows things down, then spend the last few minutes showing how we typically pair a cleaner web front-end with automated follow-up and routing — and at the end, decide together if it makes sense to scope something out or part ways. Sound fair?"

Pause for confirmation — never launch into a pitch before getting agreement.

### Phase 2: Current-State & Bottleneck Diagnosis (2:00–6:00)

Ask two or three focused questions and let them talk:

> "Walk me through what happens when a new prospect finds `[Company Name]` — whether from Google, a referral, or `[Domain.com]` — and wants to book, get a quote, or get started."

Follow-up probes:
- **Manual follow-up / inbox lag:** "When you're out on jobs, in meetings, or tied up with operations, how long does it usually take before someone actually responds or gets them on the calendar?"
- **Spreadsheet / copy-paste / re-entry:** "Once someone fills out a form, calls in, or gets approved, what has to happen manually behind the scenes before it hits your calendar, CRM, billing, or team?"
- **Inherited old systems (post-SBA acquisition):** "How much of that setup came from the previous setup versus something you built intentionally since taking over?"

### Phase 3: Quantify Cost & Ideal State (6:00–10:00)

> "Got it. Rough ballpark — how many hours a week are you or your team spending chasing leads, re-keying info, or playing phone/email tag just to get a simple inquiry scheduled or quoted?"

> "And when a warm lead sits for a few hours — or doesn't get an easy mobile option — what does losing even two or three of those a month look like in real revenue?"

> "If we waved a wand over the next 60 days, what would have to be true about your website and back-office handoffs for you to feel like it was a huge win?"

### Phase 4: Tailored Web + Automation Blueprint (10:00–13:00)

Mirror their exact words back — never generic agency fluff:

> "Here's how I'd think about solving `[their specific bottleneck, e.g., slow mobile intake + manual booking/CRM entry]` without forcing you to rip out what already works:
> 1. **Front-End Conversion:** Rebuild or tighten `[specific page/flow]` so mobile visitors can either book a slot or submit a structured request in under 60 seconds — no clunky dead-ends.
> 2. **Deterministic Automation:** The moment that comes in, wire it automatically to `[instant SMS/email confirmation + calendar/CRM routing + Slack/email alert with full context]` so nothing sits unmonitored while you're busy.
> 3. **Clean Guardrails:** Keep human review right where judgment matters, while automating the repetitive glue work."

### Phase 5: Next Step / Scope Decision (13:00–15:00)

> "We're right around the 13-minute mark. Based on what you shared, there's a clear opportunity to cut `[X hours/week]` of manual coordination and tighten up how `[Domain.com]` turns traffic into booked conversations.
> Usually the best next step from here is a 20-minute **Scope & Workflow Map** session where I walk you through the exact page layout, automation flow, fixed/tiered pricing, and timeline — or if timing or budget isn't right for `[Company Name]` right now, we can say so plainly and keep it simple. Which way are you leaning?"

## High-signal call habits

- **Take notes out loud:** "Let me jot that down — so mobile quote requests currently go to a shared inbox and get checked once or twice a day" — proves listening and builds accuracy.
- **Do not over-engineer on call one:** Naming 12 tools or debating APIs loses non-technical owners. Stick to what happens for the customer and what stops happening manually for them.
- **Respect the 15-minute mark:** Ending on time — or asking "We're at 15 minutes, want 3 more minutes to wrap or pick up Tuesday?" — builds immediate trust with busy operators.

## Next step after this call

20-minute **Scope & Workflow Map** session: exact page layout, automation flow, fixed/tiered pricing, timeline. Or a clean disqualify.
